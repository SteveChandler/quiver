### Root cause

`parseWaterTempF` (`lib/utils/wetsuit-utils.ts`) applied a sanity check rejecting
anything above **90°F**, returning `null` — indistinguishable from missing data.
`getWaterTempMetaData` then reported no temperature, `hasSubPageData` went false,
and the page stamped `noindex`.

Texas and Florida Gulf water exceeds 90°F in August. Measured against production
over the trailing 14 days:

| Value | Rows | Distinct beaches |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| 90°F | 751 | 10 |
| 91°F | 199 | 10 |
| 92°F | 2 | 1 |

**201 rows above the ceiling across 10 beaches** — 4 in TX, 6 in FL — with a
maximum of 92°F. These are physically plausible Gulf temperatures, not corrupt
data. The distribution is dense right at the boundary, so pages *flap*: whichever
forecast row is newest when the page regenerates decides whether it is indexable.

Two verification steps ruled out the alternatives before the ceiling was
confirmed:

- **Not a stale cache.** The `noindex` responses were `x-vercel-cache: HIT` with
  `age` around 150s, so they were freshly generated.
- **Not the shared predicate.** Running `getForecastIndexabilityForBeaches` and
  `isBeachSubPageIndexable` against production for all four beaches returned
  `indexable: true` on both the page-side and sitemap-side inputs, with fresh
  `NOAA_NWS` snapshots. The predicate is sound; only the temperature parse failed.

### Fix applied

The ceiling was raised to a named `MAX_PLAUSIBLE_WATER_TEMP_F = 100`, which still
catches unit errors and sentinel values while admitting real warm water. Re-running
the same production probe afterwards: **0 rejected rows, down from 201**, maximum
accepted value now 91°F.

Blast radius is contained — `parseWaterTempF` has exactly one consumer
(`lib/seo/water-temp-meta-data.ts`), and `getWetsuitRecommendation` already
treats everything at or above 72°F as boardshorts. Two tests updated, one added
pinning 91°F and 92°F as valid. 2,270 tests across 106 suites pass; `tsc` and
`eslint --max-warnings=0` clean. **Uncommitted.**

### Follow-up not fixed here

The sitemap is `force-dynamic` and re-evaluates indexability on every request,
while the sub-page routes are `force-static` with `revalidate = 3600`. The two
therefore cannot be guaranteed consistent for up to an hour after any data change,
whatever the shared predicate says. Raising the ceiling removes the current
trigger; it does not remove the asymmetry. Worth an explicit decision on whether
the sitemap should read from the same frozen evaluation the pages serve.
