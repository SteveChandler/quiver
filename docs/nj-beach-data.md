### 4. Washington Beach (Spring Lake)

- **Country:** US

- **Break Type:** beach
- **Skill Level:** beginner
- **Crowd Level:** moderate
- **Description:** Washington Beach is a designated surfing beach at the north end of Spring Lake, known as a longboard haven. Mushy, forgiving takeoffs over a sand bottom make it an ideal spot for beginners and log riders. The mellow wave quality and wide-open lineup keep the vibe relaxed, though the "log set" can create its own hassles when it gets crowded.

- **Crowd Tips:** Popular with longboarders and beginners — the "log set" can hog waves. Surf early mornings or on weekday afternoons for more space. Less crowded than Surfer's Beach due to less drive-up access.
- **Best Conditions:** Mid to high tide, W to NW wind, 2-4 ft S to SE swell. Small, clean summer days are when Washington shines as a longboard playground.
- **Access:** Walk from Washington Avenue to the beach. Designated surfing beach operates 9am-6pm. Beach badge required in summer.
- **Parking:** Street parking on Washington Avenue and surrounding blocks. No dedicated lot — limited availability in summer.

- **Description:** Seven Presidents Oceanfront Park is a 38-acre county park that was one of the first locations on the Jersey Shore to allow surfing. Named after the seven US presidents who vacationed in Long Branch, the park offers designated surf zones alongside swimming areas, a snack bar, showers, and volleyball courts. The beach break produces mellow peaks over sand with inconsistent but occasionally fun surf.

- **Best Months:** [6, 7, 8, 9, 10, 11]

- **Crowd Tips:** Even when there are waves, it is not likely to be crowded. The park setting and designated areas spread surfers out. One of the best options for an uncrowded NJ session.
- **Best Conditions:** Low incoming tide, W wind, 3-5 ft SE swell. The best conditions have historically been in summer (July) with clean surfable waves about 50% of the time.
- **Access:** 221 Ocean Avenue North, Long Branch. Enter through the park entrance. Lifeguards supervise designated surfing and swimming areas in summer.
- **Parking:** Large pay parking lot within the park. Ample spaces compared to other Jersey Shore spots. Park entrance fee applies in summer.

- **Access:** Boardwalk access at 7th Street. Designated surfing beach with lifeguard supervision in summer. Beach tags required.
- **Parking:** Street parking on 7th Street and surrounding blocks. Metered spots fill quickly in summer. Consider biking from further parking areas.

## Data Notes

### Coordinate methodology
Coordinates were determined by cross-referencing city/town center coordinates with specific street-level access points (e.g., 3rd Avenue at Ocean Avenue in Belmar), Google Maps satellite imagery for jetty/groin locations, and surf-forecast.com/Surfline break maps. Coordinates represent the primary surf zone, not the parking lot or beach access point.

### Cape May wind/swell anomaly
Cape May beaches face south to southeast, unlike the rest of NJ which faces east. This means:
- **Offshore wind** is from the N to NE (not W as in the rest of NJ)
- **Swell window** includes south swells that the rest of NJ cannot access
- **NE storms** that blow onshore elsewhere are **offshore** at Cape May
- This makes Cape May a critical alternative when the rest of the Jersey Shore is blown out

### Poverty Beach wind direction note
Poverty Beach is on the east-facing portion of the Cape May peninsula, so its offshore direction (~340° NNW) splits the difference between the south-facing Cove/Broadway spots and the east-facing rest of NJ.
