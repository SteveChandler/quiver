# Quiver Brand Guide

What Quiver is, who it's for, and how it should feel. This is the reference point for every product decision, design choice, and line of copy.

---

## Who Quiver is for

Quiver is for the surfer who wants to know where to go today without having to decode a forecast. They surf a few times a week, maybe more. They're not beginners — they know what a good session feels like — but they're not the person cross-referencing buoy data with wind models at 5am either.

Right now, they check the cams. That's the most honest source they have. The written forecast is vague. The color-coded ratings are supposed to make it simple, but a yellow star at their local break means nothing — is that "average for the coast" or "average for a spot that's usually great"? There's no way to tell. The colors flatten everything into a grade that ignores the spot, the surfer, and the day.

What actually bothers them isn't that forecasts are hard to read. It's that the forecast doesn't learn. It's wrong on Tuesday, and it's wrong the same way the following Tuesday, and there's nothing they can do about it. They can't say "this wasn't a 3-5ft day here" and have anything change. They're consuming a broadcast that doesn't know them and doesn't want to.

This person doesn't need more data. They need a better answer. One that gets sharper over time — ideally because their own experience is part of what makes it sharper.

## How Quiver should feel

Quiver should feel like something you keep. Not something you try.

The closest analog isn't another app — it's a console game on a cartridge. You put it in and it works. It doesn't need an update to be useful. It doesn't rearrange itself every time you open it. It doesn't ask you to subscribe to the version that actually works. It's the whole thing, and it's yours.

### Permanent, not disposable

Your sessions, your feedback, your history — they accumulate. Six months in, Quiver is more useful than the day you downloaded it, because it knows your coast, your spots, your patterns. It's worth the space on your phone because you've built something in it.

### Simple, not simplified

There's a difference. Simplified is taking complex data and painting over it with colors that don't mean anything. Simple is doing the hard work so the surfer gets a clear, honest answer. "It's going to be good at your spot this morning. Here's why." No decoding. No rubric to memorize.

### Warm, not corporate

It should feel like it was made by someone who surfs, not by a company that sells surfing. No stock-photo energy. No marketing voice. The tone is direct, a little informal, like a friend who checked the conditions before you woke up.

### Handmade, not generated

Every screen should feel considered. Not A/B tested into blandness, not following a template. The kind of care you can feel — the way you could feel that a small team made your favorite game, because every detail had a point of view.

## Visual identity

The visual direction draws from analog surf culture — VHS-era footage, hand-drawn sticker sheets, late-80s print ads from local shops. This applies to everything, eventually. Not just marketing materials, but the product itself.

### Color palette direction

Move away from clean corporate blue toward saturated, warm, analog tones. Deep ocean teals, electric purples, warm amber/orange — like a sunset captured on VHS. Not flat pastels, not muted minimalism. Saturated and alive, with the warmth of a CRT screen.

Black as a confident anchor — bold, not afraid of contrast. Pinks, sky blues, yellows as accent energy — the sticker sheet palette.

### Texture & surface

Nothing should feel flat or sterile. Grain, noise, texture — surfaces that feel physical. The VHS/analog quality: slightly imperfect, warm, not digitally crisp. Like a surf magazine page you've folded and kept, not a Figma export.

### Typography direction

Bold, expressive, confident — not the thin clean sans-serifs of SaaS products. Hand-drawn energy even in digital type: weight, personality, slight irregularity. The Rip Curl ad and sticker sheet lettering as north stars — unapologetic, local, fun.

Long-term: explore custom or display typefaces that feel drawn, not rendered.

### Illustration & graphics

Hand-drawn over geometric. Icons that look like someone sketched them, not pulled from a library. Every graphic element should have a point of view — no two things should look like they came from the same template. Personality over consistency. A sticker-sheet mentality: varied, playful, human.

### Photography & imagery

Analog film quality over digital perfection. Grain, warmth, motion blur. Silhouettes and action over posed lifestyle shots. The surfer in the wave, not the surfer holding a coffee at a beachside cafe.

Never stock photography. If it could be on a Surfline homepage, it doesn't belong here.

### What this is not

- Not clean minimalism (Apple, Stripe)
- Not corporate blue SaaS (Surfline, most weather apps)
- Not retro-kitsch or ironic nostalgia — the warmth is genuine, not cosplay
- Not web3/crypto aesthetic (dark mode neon gradients)

## The public pages are the product

Beach pages, city pages, forecast pages — these aren't marketing content. They're Quiver doing its job on the open web. A surfer searching "surf forecast Blacks Beach" and landing on a Quiver page should get a genuinely useful answer, not a teaser designed to push them toward an app download.

The public pages follow the same brand rules as the rest of the product: simple, honest, warm. They give a clear answer. They don't gatekeep data behind a sign-up wall. If a surfer gets what they need and leaves, that's fine — the page did its job. If they stick around and create an account, it's because the tool earned it.

The line is: **every public page should be useful on its own.** If it only exists to rank in search and funnel traffic, it doesn't belong. If it exists because a surfer would actually want that page, it does.

## What Quiver is not

**Quiver is not a media company.** No editorial content about "the best beginner boards for 2026." No lifestyle blog. No branded content partnerships. Quiver is a tool. If it's not helping you decide where to surf or remember where you surfed, it doesn't belong.

**Quiver is not a data dashboard.** The goal is never to show more data. If a surfer has to interpret a chart to make a decision, something failed. The work of translating data into a recommendation happens inside Quiver, not inside the surfer's head.

**Quiver is not a paywall with a free tier.** There's no crippled version designed to frustrate you into upgrading. The app works. All of it. If Quiver ever charges for something, it should be for something new — not for unlocking what you already had.

**Quiver is not Surfline.** Not a cheaper version of it. Not an indie alternative. Quiver doesn't compete with Surfline by doing the same thing for less money. It competes by doing a different thing entirely — giving surfers an answer instead of raw data, and getting better because surfers use it.

**Quiver is not trying to be everything.** It doesn't need a podcast. It doesn't need a merch store. If a feature doesn't make the forecast better or the session log more useful, it's noise.
