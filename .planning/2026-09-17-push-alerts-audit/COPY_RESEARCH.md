### Slang and cultural patterns

| Device | How it lands | Use note |
|---|---|---|
| Dawn patrol, glass-off | Natural and specific | Timeless. Use only when the forecast actually supports the timing. |
| Firing, going off, pumping, macking | High-energy core slang | “Pumping” is broadest; “macking” skews older/core. Never all-caps “firing” or pair it with “now.” |
| The goods | Relaxed approval | Safe and timeless when the day is genuinely good. |
| Sick day, ditch-work day | Playful weekday temptation | Use sparingly; imply, never instruct someone to deceive an employer. |
| Parking-lot check | Relatable ritual and mild self-mockery | Safe, especially when Quiver saves the user a wasted check. |
| Bring the step-up | Useful board-choice shorthand | Only when period/size/power really justify more board. |
| Crowd jokes | Universally understood | Joke about the inevitability of company, never promise an empty lineup. |
| The Wednesday swell | A midweek gift; sometimes a *Big Wednesday* wink | Safe without film knowledge, provided Wednesday is the actual peak. |
| “You should’ve been here yesterday” | Classic surfer disappointment trope | Flip it toward tomorrow; do not make Quiver sound late. |
| Surf-report radio voice | Deadpan nostalgia | Parody the cadence, not a presenter’s identifiable wording. |
| “Good to Epic” | Recognizable Surfline grading language | Avoid copying the branded phrase; make Quiver’s judgment its own. |

### Allusion safety

Allude to titles, characters, places, props and motifs. A tiny phrase such as “vaya con Dios,” “gnarly,” or “Charlie don’t surf” is the ceiling, not an invitation to quote the surrounding scene. Never reproduce longer dialogue, monologues, narration, lyrics, or a film’s distinctive sequence of lines. Prefer transformed jokes that still explain the forecast. Real injury, death, mental health, Hawaiian cultural conflict and genuinely dangerous surf are not comedy material.

## Part 4 — Rules for the pool

1. Suppress the daily call unless at least one beach has a real go window; maybe and flat days stay silent.
2. Block exact-title repeats per user for 30 days on daily calls and 120 days on swell alerts; if exhausted, use the least-recent eligible line.
